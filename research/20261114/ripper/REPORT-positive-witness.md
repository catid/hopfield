This post-hoc mechanism study reuses all 32 H1an filtered streams unchanged: the same acquired masks, observed labels and accepted rows. It changes only final pair retention. A pair is removed after any observed equal-digit coquery among accepted rows; an unexposed pair remains retained. No adaptive trajectory is changed or rerun.

The rule is conditional on the explicitly assumed shared-collapse law and supplied digit-multiplicity prior. Those assumptions establish that all accepted labels are clean. True peer cells therefore cannot have equal labels, proving true-edge retention separately from the empirical exact-recovery target. The rule is unsafe under arbitrary undetected label errors.

The independent auditor reconstructed every joint and equal count from all 35,520 observed rows, updating only accepted rows. All 103,680 pair-bank counts matched the archived fits; the final rules, unexposed-pair handling and true-edge retention were independently checked. The new rule removes 54 of the archived 54 extra edge-bank pairs.

This isolates the effect of evidence conservatism at fixed acquired data. It is not a fresh-generalization or revised adaptive-acquisition experiment. No new queries, source rows, solver inputs or GPU work were used.
